App privacy notice

Burn3d_

Last updated 19 September 2026

This notice covers the Burn3d app. The privacy policy at burn3d.com/privacy is separate, and covers the website and the waitlist only.

The short version. Burn3d needs your location to work, and it shares some of it with the person you are playing with. It never asks for your email address, phone number or a password. You can delete everything it holds from inside the app, at any time.

1. Your account

The first time you open Burn3d it creates an anonymous account — an identifier held on your phone and on our server. There is no email address, password or phone number, and nothing links it to you personally.

If you use the friends list, you also choose a name and receive a code that looks like KILN-4827.

  • Your name is visible to your friends, and to anyone you send a friend request to.
  • Your code only reaches people you hand it to. Nobody can search for you by name, and there is no way to browse other players.
  • Friends require both people to agree. Either of you can remove the other at any time, and it disappears from both lists.
  • Your status — whether the app is open, whether you are in the lobby or in a game, and when you were last seen — is visible to your friends and to nobody else.
  • Invites carry your name and a single-use room code to one friend, and expire after 60 seconds.

The same anonymous account ID travels with you into matchmaking and game rooms, so that a player you block stays blocked. Other phones searching at the same time can see that ID — a random string, never a name.

Delete my account, in the app's menu, removes the account, your name, your code, your friends list, and any requests or invites — for you, and from the lists of people who had you in theirs.

2. Location

This is the most sensitive thing Burn3d handles, so it is worth being exact about.

WhenWhat is sharedWho can see it
Looking for a round with a strangerYour approximate location, deliberately rounded to a grid of roughly 110 metresOther players currently searching, so you can be matched by rough proximity
During a round with a strangerThe agreed meeting area, and whether you have arrived — never your own positionThe player you are matched with
During a round with a friend, in one of the two rolesYour precise location, continuously, until the round endsThe friend you agreed to play with
During a round with a friend, in the other roleNothing. Your position stays on your deviceNobody
Nearby, if you switch it onWhich square of roughly 1.1 kilometres you are standing in — never a position, a distance, or a mapYour friends, and only while your own switch is on

Nearby is off until you turn it on, and it stops the moment you close the app.

Location is only collected while the app is open. Burn3d does not track you in the background, and never asks for background location permission.

3. Photos

In a round with a friend, one of you takes three photos that the other can see during that round — though the third is not always shown, depending on a choice the other player makes. All three are uploaded so they can be shown, and deleted when you leave the room. They are not used for anything else, and are never attached to an identity.

4. What you type

Short descriptions of yourself, the questions you ask, and the names you give to a meeting area. These live in the game room and are deleted with it. Please do not put anything in them you would not say out loud to a stranger — the person you are playing with will read them.

5. Reports and blocking

What you type is checked on your phone before it is sent. Slurs, sexual content and threats are refused. The check happens on the device, and nothing about it is stored or sent anywhere.

If you report another player, we receive: the reason you chose, anything you wrote, your account ID and theirs, the game room, what they had typed, and copies of any photos they took that you saw. The photos are copied because the originals are deleted when the round ends, and a report about a photo would otherwise have nothing to look at. The copies cannot be viewed from the app by anyone, including you.

Reports are read by us and used only to deal with what was reported.

If you block a player, they leave your friends list, cannot send you requests or invites, and are never matched with you again. They are not told.

6. Round records and feedback

When a round ends we keep a record of how it went: the area name and size, a deliberately coarsened centre point, how long things took, and the outcome. It tells us whether the game is working — for instance, whether an area was too large to search in the time allowed. It is not linked to a person, and holds no photos or messages.

Rounds with a friend also record how well the GPS performed: accuracy in metres, and how long each state lasted. No coordinates are stored.

If you send feedback at the end of a round, we receive what you wrote along with those round details.

7. Who else handles it

  • Google Firebase — stores accounts, friends lists, game rooms and photos. Data is held on servers in Singapore and Australia.
  • Google Forms — receives feedback and reports you choose to send.

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with advertisers.

8. How long it is kept

  • Your account, name, code and friends list — until you delete your account.
  • Your status and Nearby square — replaced as they change, and cleared when you close the app.
  • Invites — 60 seconds.
  • Game rooms, photos, and anything you typed — deleted when you leave the round. Rooms abandoned without leaving properly are cleared automatically after three hours.
  • Round records, feedback and reports — up to 24 months, then deleted.
  • Photos copied into a report — 90 days, then deleted automatically.
  • Your block list — until you delete your account.

9. Your choices

  • Nearby is off unless you turn it on, and can be turned off again whenever you like.
  • Contact preference lets you ask the player who finds you not to make contact beyond the signal itself.
  • Report and block are wherever another player's content appears — their profile in a round, their photos and questions, the end of a round, and a long press on anyone in your friends list.
  • Delete my account is in the app's menu, and needs no request to us.
  • Location and camera permissions can be withdrawn in your phone's settings. Burn3d needs location to work, so most of the game stops without it.

10. Age

Burn3d arranges meetings between people in public places. You must be at least 18 years old to take part. It is not intended for children.

11. Safety

Burn3d is a game played in public. Meet in busy, public places, tell someone where you are going, and leave if you feel uncomfortable. Nothing in this notice — and nothing in the app's design — removes your own judgement about your safety.
